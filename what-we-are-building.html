In Nigeria, too many condolences come too soon.

Too many families have said, “But he was fine yesterday.” Too many people have lost someone and spent years wondering whether there was something they could have noticed earlier.

Not every death can be prevented. But some families could have had more time.

That thought is at the heart of Mediqit.

A 2021 analysis estimated that 27.5 million Nigerian adults were living with hypertension in 2020. Roughly seven in ten did not know they had it. Fewer than three in every hundred had their blood pressure under control.

These are our parents, neighbours, colleagues and friends. People getting up early, sitting in traffic, running businesses and looking after everybody else. People who feel fine and have no obvious reason to think anything is wrong.

High blood pressure often has no noticeable symptoms. Sometimes, a person only learns about it after a stroke, kidney damage or heart failure. By then, the family is trying to understand what happened while worrying about what comes next.

Being young does not make someone immune. In a study of 71 stroke patients aged 18 to 40 at two hospitals in Kano, almost three out of four had hypertension. Published in 2012, the study does not represent every young Nigerian. But it is a reminder that these problems can affect people who believe they still have age on their side.

Diabetes can go unnoticed, too. The International Diabetes Federation estimated that nearly four in five Nigerian adults aged 20 to 79 living with diabetes in 2024 had not been diagnosed.

It is frightening to think that someone you love could be living with a condition neither of you knows about. It is harder when you live far away and “How is your health?” is a question you can only ask over the phone.

Then there are our mothers.

Nigeria had an estimated 75,000 maternal deaths in 2023, more than any other country. Seventy-five thousand women. Each one belonged to a family that expected her to come home.

Blood pressure problems in pregnancy are among the dangers that need close attention. Regular blood pressure checks, urine tests and other antenatal assessments help healthcare professionals identify pre-eclampsia and respond. A home monitor can support that care when a clinician recommends it. It cannot replace an antenatal appointment.

And anyone who has sat beside a sick child knows how long a night can feel.

You touch their forehead again. You watch their breathing. You wonder whether they are improving or whether you should already be on your way to the hospital.

Some important signs cannot be judged reliably by looking. Research across 12 hospitals in southwest Nigeria found that low blood oxygen was associated with substantially higher odds of death among hospitalised newborns and children. Clinical signs alone often failed to identify it.

Parents should not have to become doctors to care for their children. A son or daughter should not need a medical degree to help an ageing parent.

But families deserve access to suitable tools, instructions they can understand and professional help when something is wrong.

We know that a device alone cannot solve the problem. Sometimes the barrier is the hospital bill. Sometimes it is the distance, the transport fare or the fear of what a test might reveal. Sometimes a person has been trying to get help and has simply not received it.

Families already carry enough worry. We want to make one part of caring for each other a little easier.

THAT IS WHY WE ARE BUILDING MEDIQIT.

Mediqit is a Nigerian health commerce platform being built to help households and caregivers find and obtain everyday health monitors and medical supplies.

Our planned products include:

  • Blood pressure monitors
  • Glucometers, test strips and compatible supplies
  • Thermometers
  • Pulse oximeters
  • First aid kits and wound care supplies
  • Clinical equipment for medical students

And more, as we grow and learn what our customers need.

We want someone buying a monitor for their mother to understand what they are paying for. What comes in the box? Which accessories will she need? Can she follow the instructions? Who can they contact with a product question?

Whether you live with the person you are caring for or send money home from another city, those details matter. Buying something for their health should leave you feeling better informed, not more uncertain.

We are not replacing doctors, nurses, pharmacists or laboratories. We need them, and so do the families we hope to serve.

A reading is not a diagnosis. But it could be the reason someone you love speaks to a doctor today instead of putting it off again.

And if someone is seriously sick, a normal reading should never be a reason to delay medical care.

We cannot promise that every emergency will give a warning or that owning a monitor will prevent a loss. We can build a service that takes these purchases seriously, because we understand why people make them.

Behind an order for a blood pressure monitor may be a daughter worried about her father. Behind a thermometer may be a parent caring for a sick child. Behind a first aid kit may be someone trying to make their home a little safer.

Those are the people we are building Mediqit for.
