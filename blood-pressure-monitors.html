What to consider before choosing one.

A suitable monitor should be easy to position, simple to read and appropriate for the person using it. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions and any guidance from a qualified healthcare professional.

01 · CUFF

Check cuff fit

The cuff should match the user's stated arm-size range. A poor fit may affect the usefulness of readings.

02 · DISPLAY

Prioritise readability

Clear numbers, helpful indicators and simple controls matter, particularly for older adults and caregivers.

03 · RECORDS

Consider memory features

Saved readings, user profiles and date records can make routine tracking easier when used as instructed.

Common questions

Which type is commonly used at home?

Many people use automatic upper-arm monitors because they are designed for straightforward home operation. Suitability still depends on cuff fit, instructions and professional guidance.

Can a monitor diagnose a health condition?

No. A device provides measurements; it does not replace diagnosis or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional. Seek appropriate care for symptoms or concerning readings.
